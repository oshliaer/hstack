## Decision question (reframed)

> "Is the demand in the new location sufficient to cover a $25K/month debt service within 12 months, given our current operational bandwidth?"

**Original question:** "Should I take a $500K loan to expand the business?"

*Reframing shifted focus from the loan itself to the demand and execution constraints — the real uncertainties.*

---

## Options considered

| Option | Commits to | Forecloses | Score /5 |
|--------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| A: Take loan, open Location 2 | $500K debt, 5 new hires, 12-month ramp | Ability to pivot quickly if market shifts | 3.1 |
| B: Open Location 2 with equity round | Dilution, slower process (3–6 months) | Debt burden; faster scale if oversubscribed | 2.8 |
| C: Pilot with pop-up (3 months, $40K) | Market test commitment | None significant | 3.7 |
| Do nothing | Current trajectory | Cedes market to incoming competitor | 2.2 |

## Key evidence

**From /reframe:**
- The root motivation (5 Whys) is market-share defense, not growth — a competitor is rumored to be entering the market.
- The loan amount ($500K) was chosen before validating whether Location 2 would be profitable.
- Alternative framing: "Is our competitive moat durable enough that inaction is safe for 18 months?"

**From /source-audit:**
- Market growth rate (20% YoY) rated YELLOW — sourced from a 3-year-old industry report.
- Location 2 foot traffic estimate is UNANCHORED — based on founder intuition, no primary data.
- Current-location unit economics are GREEN — 18 months of P&L data confirms 34% EBITDA margin.

**From /scenario:**
- Base case: break-even at month 14, EV of loan +$180K at month 24.
- Pessimistic (35% weight): break-even pushed to month 22, cash falls to 2-month runway.
- Key driver: ramp speed. Every month of delay costs $25K in covered debt service.

**From /pre-mortem:**
- CRITICAL failure mode: Location 2 ramp takes 18+ months instead of 12, creating a cash crisis.
- CRITICAL failure mode: Key manager leaves during transition; execution quality drops.
- Non-negotiable: minimum $60K/month revenue from Location 2 by month 9 or halt.

**From /stress-test:**
- Max loss (zero-return): $600K over 24 months (loan + interest).
- Cash runway drops from 8 months to 3.5 months under pessimistic scenario.
- Leverage variable: hiring the Location 2 manager 60 days before opening improves break-even by 1.8 months.

---

## Recommendation

**Decision:** Option C — Run a 3-month pop-up pilot at $40K to validate Location 2 demand before committing to the loan.

**Confidence:** 62% — MEDIUM

**Decision type:** Partially reversible

*The loan (Option A) has an acceptable expected value but an unacceptable pessimistic downside given the current 3.5-month runway in the worst case. Option C resolves the highest-uncertainty claim (Location 2 demand) in 90 days at 8% of the loan cost, while holding the option open.*

---

## Strongest counter-argument

> The competitor entering the market in the next 6 months makes a 3-month delay costly. By the time the pilot completes and the loan closes (add 45 days for processing), we may have ceded first-mover advantage in the new location. The competitor has deeper pockets, and if they open first, the unit economics that justify the loan may never materialize. Speed is the actual variable, and the pilot sacrifices it.

**Why proceeding despite it:**
The competitor's market entry is a GUESSED assumption (rated Unknown in `/source-audit`). Betting $500K on an unverified threat is not rational. The pilot includes a competitive monitoring trigger: if competitor breaks ground within 60 days, escalate immediately to Option A without completing the full pilot.
